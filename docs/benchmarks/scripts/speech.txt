Fifty years ago, just under a mile from here, a group of people did something extraordinary. The country it created wasn't perfect either, but it set us on this path. This beautiful, audacious, complicated path that is still evolving today. And the Declaration of Independence still gives me chills when I read it aloud. We hold these truths to be self-evident, that all are created equal, that they are endowed with certain unalienable rights. So a couple of years ago, a philanthropic foundation in the United States asked me a question. I interviewed civic leaders in West Africa and scholars in Eastern Europe and democracy activists across four continents. These leaders that are pushing people and places away from democracy, what is it that makes their message so effective? Pretty much everyone I talked to said I was asking the wrong question. They said that authoritarianism was not surging because of anything it was doing right, but because of what democracy was getting wrong. That stopped me in my tracks. The way they put it, authoritarianism was like an opportunistic disease that takes hold when democracy was weak.
